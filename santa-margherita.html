The deeper-research version

Portofino, Camogli & one secret upgrade

Portofino and Camogli are both worth our time for different reasons. San Fruttuoso is the wildcard: potentially the most memorable boat day, but also the least reliable in October.

Iconic honeymoon memory

Portofino — go later

⛴️ Ferry out🚌 Bus backLate afternoon

Why I changed the timing: Portofino is more romantic when the day-trippers are thinning and the light gets soft. We do not need to be there at 9 AM.

  • Ferry: Line 1 runs almost all year; Santa → Portofino is €12 one way / €20 round trip. Same-day departures can be cancelled for sea conditions, low demand or minimum-passenger rules.
  • Castello Brown: €10, open 10 AM–6 PM throughout October; about a 10-minute uphill walk from the harbor.
  • Lighthouse: Il Faro is open daily 10:30 AM to sunset, weather permitting. This is the romantic aperitivo option.
  • Sunset Oct 12: about 6:44 PM.
  • Return: bus 782 after sunset. The 2026–27 winter timetable is active during our stay; the dedicated single ticket is currently €5.

Camogli — go for sunset

🚆 Direct trainMinutes awayLate afternoon

Why it moved later in the day: Camogli faces west. Its tall pastel waterfront and harbor make more sense at golden hour than as another midday sightseeing stop.

  • Direct trains operate between Camogli-San Fruttuoso and Santa Margherita on the published 2026 timetable.
  • No taxi, bus transfer or reservation needed.
  • Walk the waterfront, harbor and old lanes; skip the steep San Rocco climb unless we actively want a hike.
  • Sunset Oct 13: roughly 6:42 PM in the Santa/Camogli area.
  • Either return for Zi’Ninella in Santa or stay for Izoa if we want a longer night.

Perfect-weather upgrade: San Fruttuoso + Portofino

If Oct 12 is calm and the Line 1 schedule is good, this is the one upgrade I would seriously consider. The 2026 fare from Santa Margherita to Portofino + San Fruttuoso is €24 round trip, and the same ticket allows a stop in Portofino. The Abbey of San Fruttuoso is open every day in October from 10 AM to 3:45 PM and costs €10. The tradeoff is reliability: boats can be reduced or cancelled for weather, low demand and minimum passenger counts, so this should be a morning-of decision—not something we build the honeymoon around.

My ranking after the deeper research: 1) Portofino late afternoon/sunset for the iconic honeymoon memory; 2) Camogli late afternoon/sunset for the place we are more likely to enjoy lingering; 3) San Fruttuoso only as a perfect-weather bonus. I would not spend a full day in Portofino.

Three towns, three personalities

History & interesting facts

Santa, Portofino and Camogli look like one postcard coastline today, but they grew out of fishing, maritime trade, defensive forts and very different relationships with tourism.

🏘️ Santa Margherita wasn't always one town

The area developed around separate villages including Pescino and Corte. In 1813, under Napoleon, they were unified as “Porto Napoleone”; the modern Santa Margherita name was confirmed later in the 19th century.

🌿 Villa Durazzo began as a summer estate

The Durazzo family built the villa in 1678. Its gardens, sea loggia and later grand-hotel history capture Santa's shift from coastal town to elegant resort.

🚂 The railway changed Santa's destiny

The railway and new road toward Portofino opened the coastline to travelers and triggered the age of grand hotels and villas that still defines Santa's look.

🚤 Portofino was genuinely isolated

Until the late 19th century, Portofino could essentially be reached only by sea or difficult hill paths. A carriage road finally began breaking that isolation in the 1880s.

🏰 An English consul helped make Portofino fashionable

Montagu Brown acquired the old San Giorgio fortress—today Castello Brown—and his hospitality helped draw British and Genoese high society to what had been a fishing village.

⛵ Camogli was the “Town of a Thousand White Sailboats”

Its 17th–19th century maritime tradition produced shipowners and trading vessels far beyond what the village's small size suggests today.

🎨 Camogli's facades are part architecture, part illusion

Like much of Liguria, buildings use trompe-l'oeil painted architectural details—false cornices, stonework and even shutters—to make tall coastal facades more elaborate.

🙏 Madonna dei Gotti is a sailors' tradition

The Camogli festival centers on a votive shrine to the Madonna of the Good Voyage, protector of fishermen and sailors; the “Gotti” nickname recalls the old taverns around the harbor.
