Our Verona plan

A relaxed 2½-day itinerary

Thu • Oct 1

Arrive, settle in, fall in love with the city

After the 12:45 PM flight, keep the first afternoon deliberately easy.

  • Taxi to the apartment, drop luggage and reset.
  • Walk Piazza Bra → Via Mazzini → Piazza delle Erbe → Ponte Pietra.
  • Take the funicular to Castel San Pietro for sunset if energy is good.
  • Easy dinner close to the old center; do not commit to the 9 PM Arena show on arrival night.
Fri • Oct 2

Roman Verona + Castelvecchio + Arena night

This is the best day for the classic Verona experience.

  • Morning: enter the Arena early, before its 3 PM event-day closure.
  • Walk Castelvecchio and the fortified bridge; add San Zeno if we still want more history.
  • Late afternoon: aperitivo and a light dinner.
  • 9:00 PM: Notre Dame de Paris inside the Arena — my top event pick for the trip.
Sat • Oct 3

Local history + slow food day

Use the official English tour as the backbone, then stop scheduling things.

  • 10:30 AM: 90-minute Historical Verona city tour.
  • Lunch at Salumeria Gironda near Ponte Pietra for local cheese, salumi and a river view.
  • Choose one: Torre dei Lamberti, Juliet's courtyard/house, Dorothea Lange exhibition, or a long wine-bar afternoon.
  • Make this our best traditional dinner night.
Sun • Oct 4

Breakfast, one last walk, Parma

Checkout is by 11 AM, so skip the 10:30 history tour and avoid turning departure morning into a sprint.

  • Coffee + pastry, short walk around Piazza Bra or Porta Borsari.
  • Pick up one small Verona food gift if we have room.
  • Return for bags and head to Verona Porta Nuova for the relaxed Parma train.

Tickets & practical choices

Verona Card: probably yes, if we do the sights

From Sept. 1, 2026 the official card is €33 for 24 hours / €38 for 48 hours and includes major sights plus urban buses. I would activate a 48-hour card Friday morning only if we plan to do several paid attractions: Arena + Castelvecchio + Torre dei Lamberti + Juliet's House/San Zeno. If we mostly wander, eat and attend the musical, pay individually instead.

What to eat in Verona

Priority: risotto all'Amarone, bigoli with duck or another local ragù, bollito with pearà, local salumi and Monte Veronese cheese.

Adventurous: pastissada de caval is a deeply traditional horse-meat stew. Try it only if that sounds genuinely appealing—there is no reason to force a “local specialty.”

What to drink

Valpolicella Classico/Superiore for the easy local red; Ripasso for richer body; Amarone for the famous splurge; Recioto for sweet red dessert wine; Soave for a local white.

Bring a little Verona with us

What to taste or buy

Risini or Torta Russa

Better Verona-specific pastry souvenirs than buying generic sweets. Risini are little rice-filled tarts; Torta Russa is richer with almonds, amaretti and cocoa.

One bottle of local wine

If suitcase weight permits, pick one bottle we genuinely loved—Valpolicella, Ripasso or Amarone—rather than carrying several bottles through the rest of the trip.

Verona-roasted coffee

BASƎ / Garage Coffee Bros. near Castelvecchio is an easy, compact gift that travels far better than cheese or salumi.

Pandoro — only if we find a good one

Pandoro was invented in Verona, but it is primarily a Christmas-season cake. If a quality bakery has it in early October, great; otherwise don't chase a supermarket box just to say we bought one.

The context behind the streets

History

Scaliger Verona13th–14th centuries • Della Scala rule, Castelvecchio, fortified bridges and Dante
Venetian rule1405–1797 • Verona becomes a key fortified city of the Republic of Venice
Austrian era19th century • military importance continues under the Habsburgs
Kingdom of Italy1866 • Verona joins unified Italy
UNESCO2000 • historic Verona is inscribed as a World Heritage Site
🏟️ The Arena was enormous even by Roman standards

Built around the middle of the 1st century AD, it could hold about 30,000 spectators and originally stood outside the Roman city walls so crowds could flow in without clogging the center. Gladiator fights and animal hunts were among its ancient spectacles.

🌉 Ponte Pietra is ancient—but also a reconstruction story

The oldest bridge site in Verona dates to the Roman period. Retreating German troops blew the bridge up in 1945; Verona later reconstructed it, recovering original stones and bricks from the Adige.

🏰 Castelvecchio's bridge doubled as an emergency exit

Cangrande II della Scala had the fortified bridge built in the 1350s as a protected escape route toward Tyrol if political enemies or a revolt made the city unsafe.

📖 Dante actually lived here

Dante spent roughly seven years in Verona across two stays after his exile from Florence. Cangrande della Scala became his patron, and Dante wrote a substantial part of Paradiso while in Verona.

❤️ Juliet's balcony is not medieval Romeo-and-Juliet evidence

The house itself is medieval, but the famous balcony was added during a 1939–40 restoration using medieval stonework. The romance is literary—but families named Montecchi and Cappelletti really did exist in medieval Verona.

🧭 We are still walking the Roman city plan

UNESCO notes that Verona's ancient Roman street pattern remains legible in today's historic center. That is part of why the city feels unusually coherent even though its buildings span many centuries.
