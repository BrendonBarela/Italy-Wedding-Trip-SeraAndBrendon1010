Food is the destination here

Eat & drink

Parma is a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy. This section is deliberately more specific than the other city food guides.

Classic Parma • reserve

Ristorante Cocchi

My first choice for one traditional “greatest hits” meal. Michelin still lists it for Emilian cooking, and recent local/traveler reviews consistently praise the old-school service and Parma specialties.

Order: salumi + torta fritta, tortelli d'erbetta, anolini in brodo, and—because we're there in October—the bollito cart if available.

Destination meal • Sun/Mon only for us

Trattoria Ai Due Platani

One of the most consistently praised food destinations around Parma and Michelin-listed. It's outside the center and notoriously reservation-heavy. Their current schedule is closed Tuesday and Wednesday, so Sunday or Monday is our window.

Order: fresh stuffed pasta, salumi/torta fritta, and save room for the famous tableside gelato.

Quick lunch • Tuesday best fit

Da Pepèn

A tiny, very busy Parma panino institution. Go for speed and local energy, not a relaxed sit-down lunch. Lines are normal.

Order: prosciutto or roast-beef panino, or La Carciofa. Pesto di cavallo is the famous adventurous local option only if horse meat genuinely appeals.

Central sampler • easy logistics

Osteria dei Servi

A useful first-timer choice when we want local specialties without leaving the center. Strong on salumi, Parmigiano tastings, fresh pasta and grilled meats.

Order: torta fritta + local salumi, a Parmigiano tasting, tortelli d'erbetta or anolini.

What to eat in Parma

Priority: Parmigiano Reggiano at different ages, Prosciutto di Parma, torta fritta, tortelli d'erbetta, anolini in brodo, Salame Felino and Spalla Cotta di San Secondo.

If we see it: Culatello di Zibello is from the wider Parma province and absolutely worth tasting.

What to drink

Local Lambrusco is the obvious partner for rich salumi and torta fritta. Malvasia dei Colli di Parma is the local aromatic white to try with lighter foods or aperitivo.

Pack-light souvenirs

What to taste or buy

🧀 Vacuum-packed Parmigiano

Choose an age we actually liked—24, 30 or 36+ months—and keep the quantity small. This is the best Parma souvenir for our luggage.

🌸 Violette di Parma

Candied violets or violet sweets connect directly to Maria Luigia and weigh almost nothing.

🍷 Local bottle

Only if we love it: a Colli di Parma Malvasia or local red. We still have France and a flight home, so one bottle is enough.

🥩 Salumi for the trip

Buy a small amount to eat during the Italy portion rather than loading the suitcase with cured meat. Parma's official food shops can vacuum-pack products.

More than ham and cheese

History & interesting facts

Parma's city center is a stack of Roman streets, medieval sculpture, Renaissance painting, ducal ambition, opera culture and modern food identity.

Medieval city11th–13th c. • Cathedral, Antelami and the Baptistery
Farnese Duchy1545–1731 • Pilotta and court culture
Maria Luigia1816–1847 • Napoleon's second wife rules Parma
United Italy1860s • Duchy becomes part of Italy
Food City2015 • UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy
🏛️ Piazza Garibaldi is roughly where the Roman forum was

Parma became a Roman colony in 183 BCE, and the Roman street grid still helps explain today's center: the Via Emilia crossed the city while the forum occupied the area around modern Piazza Garibaldi.

⛪ An earthquake helped create the Cathedral we see today

The Cathedral was damaged by the 1117 earthquake and rebuilt in the 12th century. Centuries later Correggio painted its famous illusionistic Assumption dome in 1526.

👣 The Baptistery has a “devil's footprint” legend

Benedetto Antelami began the Baptistery in 1196. Local legend points to a mark on the southeast side as the footprint left when the devil tried—and failed—to kick the building down.

🎭 Teatro Farnese was built for a guest who never arrived

The huge wooden theater was created to impress Cosimo II de' Medici, but his planned visit was cancelled. The theater had to wait until 1628 for its inauguration.

💣 The Farnese Theatre is also a reconstruction story

Bombing in 1944 almost completely destroyed it. Restoration began in the 1950s, reusing surviving original wood where possible, and the reconstructed theater reopened as part of the Pilotta.

👑 Parma was ruled by Napoleon's second wife

Maria Luigia of Austria became Duchess of Parma after Napoleon's fall and ruled from 1816 to 1847. Parma still associates her with its pale “Parma yellow” architecture and beloved violets.

🎨 Correggio helped invent the visual language of the Baroque

His illusionistic domes in Parma—especially the Cathedral—use radical foreshortening so figures seem to project into the viewer's space. Later Baroque painters learned from it.

🧀 Parma's food identity is officially part of the city story

UNESCO named Parma a Creative City of Gastronomy in 2015. The agri-food sector isn't just tourism branding; it remains a major part of the local economy and culture.
